# Colin McNab

**Colin Anderson McNab** (born 3 February 1961 in Dundee, Scotland) is a Scottish chess Grandmaster, mathematician, author, problem-solving master, and endgame expert. He is Scotland's second Grandmaster, achieving the title in 1992 shortly after Paul Motwani.

## Career Overview
McNab emerged as one of Scotland's top players in the late 1970s and 1980s, claiming his first major international junior victory at the Hallsberg tournament (1979–80) and earning the International Master (IM) title in 1984. He fulfilled the requirements for the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1992. Notably, McNab held the unique distinction of obtaining the GM title via an intermediate rating calculation before officially reaching a published FIDE rating of 2500 six years later in January 1998. In addition to over-the-board chess, McNab is exceptionally versatile, holding titles as a Senior International Correspondence Chess Master (1999) and International Solving Master (2007). Outside of competitive chess, he earned a DPhil in Mathematics from the University of Oxford under the supervision of Peter Neumann.

## International and Tournament Successes
* **Commonwealth Championship:** Claimed individual gold at the 1992 Commonwealth Chess Championship in Malaysia on tiebreak, finishing ahead of an elite international field that included Tony Miles, Stuart Conquest, Mihai Suba, and William Watson.
* **Scottish National Championships:** Won the Scottish Chess Championship four times (1983, 1991, 1993, and 1995).
* **Record Olympiad Representation:** Set a Scottish record by representing his country at 18 Chess Olympiads between 1980 and 2016, consistently playing top boards. He also represented Scotland at multiple European Team Chess Championships.
* **Chess Problem Solving:** Demonstrated world-class solving skill by winning back-to-back titles at the British Chess Problem Solving Championship in 2012 and 2013.
* **Open Tournament Successes:** Took first prize at the Isle of Man International (1993) and won or shared first place at numerous events across the UK and Europe, including the Coulsdon GM tournament (2005, 2006) and joint second at the Hastings International Chess Congress (2003/04).

## Playing Style, Writing, and Publications
Renowned for his patience, positional precision, and profound endgame technique, McNab is particularly recognized for his hypermodern opening choices—often utilizing flank openings and fianchetto systems as White and the Pirc/Modern Defences as Black:
* **Endgame Columnist:** Contributed a long-running, widely studied regular column dedicated to practical and theoretical endgame analysis for *Scottish Chess* magazine.
* **Television Feature:** Profiled alongside Paul Motwani in the 1992 Grampian Television documentary *The Grandmasters of Dundee*.
* **Authored Books:**
  * *The Fianchetto King's Indian* (1996)
  * *The Ultimate Pirc* (1998, co-authored with GM John Nunn)
  * *Dangerous Weapons: The Pirc and Modern* (2009, co-authored with Richard Palliser and James Vigus)
