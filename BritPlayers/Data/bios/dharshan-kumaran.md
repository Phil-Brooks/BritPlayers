# Dharshan Kumaran

**Dharshan Kumaran** (born 7 June 1975) is an English chess Grandmaster, two-time World Youth Champion, cognitive neuroscientist, and artificial intelligence researcher at Google DeepMind.

## Career Overview
Kumaran was one of England's most gifted chess prodigies during the golden era of British junior chess in the 1980s and 1990s. Demonstrating extraordinary tactical and intuitive gifts from a young age, he won two world youth titles—the World Under-12 Championship in 1986 and the World Under-16 Championship in 1991. He earned the International Master (IM) title in 1993 and was awarded the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1997. Kumaran achieved a peak FIDE rating of 2505 in January 1995. Choosing not to pursue chess as a full-time professional career, he retired from competitive ranked tournaments to enter medicine and neuroscience. Today, he is a Staff Scientist at Google DeepMind and an Honorary Research Associate at University College London (UCL), where he has played a pivotal role in historic breakthroughs connecting neuroscience and artificial intelligence.

## International and Tournament Successes
* **Dual World Youth Titles:** Won individual world titles at two separate age levels:
  * **World Under-12 Champion** (1986, San Juan, Puerto Rico)
  * **World Under-16 Champion** (1991, Guarapuava, Brazil)
* **World Junior Championship Podium:** Tied for 3rd place at the 1994 World Junior (Under-20) Championship in Caacupé/Matinhos.
* **National Success:** Shared first place at the 1993 British Chess Championship in Dundee alongside Michael Hennigan.
* **Varsity & Open Tournaments:** Led Oxford University to multiple Varsity match victories over Cambridge in the mid-1990s, and claimed first prize at the 1995 Wagner Memorial in Poland.

## Neuroscience and Artificial Intelligence
Kumaran successfully transitioned from elite chess to world-class scientific research, focusing on the neural mechanisms of human memory and learning to inspire new AI architectures:
* **Google DeepMind Staff Scientist:** Conducts groundbreaking research on episodic memory, the hippocampus, reinforcement learning, and overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks.
* **Landmark Scientific Publications:** Co-authored some of the most influential machine-learning papers of the modern era:
  * *"Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning"* (*Nature*, 2015) – The seminal paper introducing Deep Q-Networks (DQN) capable of mastering Atari games directly from raw pixel input (cited tens of thousands of times).
  * *"Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm"* (*Science*, 2018) – Key co-author alongside Demis Hassabis, David Silver, and others on the development of **AlphaZero**, which mastered chess without human opening books or heuristic evaluation functions.
  * *"Neuroscience-Inspired Artificial Intelligence"* (*Neuron*, 2017) – A seminal review outlining how mammalian cognitive frameworks guide the design of artificial general intelligence.
