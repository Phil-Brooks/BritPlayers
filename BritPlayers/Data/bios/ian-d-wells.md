# Ian D. Wells

**Ian Duncan Wells** (22 June 1964 – 25 January 1982) was an exceptional British chess prodigy from Cumbria, England. Regarded alongside Nigel Short and Julian Hodgson as one of the top young talents of his generation, his promising career was tragically cut short at the age of 17.

## Chess Career & Achievements

* **Early Start:** Began playing local league chess at the age of 10 and was competing in tournament brackets two age groups older by the time he was 12.
* **Defeating a Legend:** In 1979, at just 14 or 15 years old, he won a notable match against the legendary Soviet Grandmaster Alexander Kotov with a score of +2 -1.
* **Master Title:** Officially qualified for the FIDE Master (FM) title at the 1981 Benedictine Open in Manchester.
* **Elite Placement:** At the 1981 Arc Young Masters, he tied for first/equal 7th in a prestigious field that put him level with Grandmaster John Nunn.

## Tragedy in Rio

In January 1982, Wells travelled to Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, to compete in an international chess tournament. After going for a swim off a local beach, he suffered a severe drowning accident. He slipped into a coma and tragically passed away six days later on 25 January 1982.

His games remain a testament to his attacking flair, deep positional feel, and the extraordinary potential of a player on the verge of international mastership.
