# James Plaskett

**Harold James Plaskett** (born 18 March 1960 in Dhekelia, Cyprus) is an English chess Grandmaster, author, and television quiz winner.

## Career Overview
Educated at Bedford Modern School, Plaskett emerged as one of England's leading junior prospects in the late 1970s, finishing third at the European Junior Chess Championship (1978/79). He earned the International Master (IM) title in 1981 and attained the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1985. In 1990, Plaskett won the **British Chess Championship** with a commanding score of 9/11. Known for his sharp, uncompromising tactical play, he served as a regular chess columnist for *The New Statesman* during the 1990s and has authored several opening manuals and instructional chess books. In 2002, he relocated with his family to Cartagena, Spain, where he continues to compete.

## Chess Highlights and "Plaskett's Puzzle"
* **Grandmaster Norms:** Earned his GM norms by winning the EEC Open in Paris (June 1983, 8/9), the 6th Benedictine International in Manchester (September 1983, 7/9), and tying for first place at the Järvenpää International in Finland (July 1985, 7/9).
* **Tournament Highlights:** Competed in numerous elite tournaments, finishing runner-up at the 1986/87 Hastings International Chess Congress (7/13) and sharing 1st–4th place at the 1986 Lugano Open alongside Viktor Korchnoi and Nigel Short.
* **"Plaskett's Puzzle":** At a super-tournament in Brussels in 1987, Plaskett introduced an intricate endgame composition originally composed around 1970 by Dutch composer Gijs van Breukelen. The position famously stumped nearly every top grandmaster present—with former World Champion Mikhail Tal reportedly the only one to solve it—and subsequently became famous throughout the chess world under the name "Plaskett's Puzzle".
* **Chess Authorship:** A prolific writer on chess openings and tactics, his works include *The English Defence*, *The Sicilian Taimanov*, *Can You Be a Tactical Chess Genius?*, *The Scandinavian Defence*, and *The Queen's Bishop Attack Revealed*.

## *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?* and *Bad Show*
* **Game Show Success:** After several attempts in the qualifying stages, Plaskett appeared in the hot seat of *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?* in January 2006 (accompanied by friend and fellow Grandmaster Stuart Conquest). He became only the seventh contestant in the UK show's history to reach £125,000 without using any lifelines, ultimately walking away with **£250,000**.
* **Ingram Cheating Case & *Quiz*:** Plaskett became a prominent public defender of Charles and Diana Ingram and Tecwen Whittock following their 2003 conviction for fraud on the show. He argued that the coughing sounds central to the prosecution were unconscious, nervous reactions common in high-stress quiz environments. In 2015, Plaskett and investigative journalist Bob Woffinden published *Bad Show: The Quiz, The Cough, The Millionaire Major*, arguing for the couple's innocence. The book served as the primary basis for James Graham's acclaimed West End stage play and the subsequent 2020 ITV/AMC television drama series, *Quiz*.

## Other Pursuits and Personal Life
* **Synchronicity and Coincidences:** Plaskett has maintained a lifelong interest in anomalous phenomena and coincidences, which he began methodically logging in the 1980s. He authored the semi-autobiographical book *Coincidences* (2000) and documented examples on his blog, *Snapshots: A Coincidence Diary*.
* **Cryptid Research:** In August 1999, Plaskett organised and led a three-week expedition in the waters off Bermuda to search for the legendary cryptid "Giant Octopus", backed by Demon Internet founder Cliff Stanford and working alongside renowned underwater *National Geographic* photographer Emory Kristof.
* **Personal Life:** Plaskett is married to the British poet, novelist, and travel writer Fiona Pitt-Kethley. They moved from Hastings to Cartagena, Spain, in 2002.
