# Jon Speelman

**Jonathan Simon Speelman** (born 2 October 1956 in London, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, author, and journalist who reached the semi-finals of the World Chess Championship Candidates Tournament.

## Career Overview
Speelman was educated at St Paul's School and read mathematics at Worcester College, Oxford. He won the British Chess Championship three times (1978, 1985, and 1986). He earned the International Master (IM) title in 1978 and became a Grandmaster (GM) in 1980. Celebrated for an imaginative, unorthodox playing style and profound endgame knowledge, Speelman climbed into the world's elite during the late 1980s, reaching a peak ranking of World No. 5 with a 2645 Elo rating.

## International Competition
* **World Championship Candidates:** Qualified for the Candidates Tournament by finishing third at the 1987 Subotica Interzonal. In the 1988–1990 cycle, he defeated Yasser Seirawan (4–1) and Nigel Short (3½–1½) to advance to the semi-finals, before narrowly falling to Jan Timman (4½–3½). He qualified again for the 1991–1993 cycle, losing a close match to eventual challenger Nigel Short.
* **Chess Olympiads:** A core pillar of the English national squad across 14 Olympiads between 1980 and 2006. He contributed to England winning team **silver medals** in 1984, 1986, and 1988, and a team **bronze medal** in 1990, alongside winning an individual silver medal on board four at Valletta (1980).
* **Tournament Successes:** Won or tied for first at numerous elite international competitions, including the Hastings International Chess Congress (1983/84 and 1986/87). In 1989, he defeated reigning World Champion Garry Kasparov en route to winning the televised BBC speed tournament.

## Writing and Journalism
Speelman is recognized as one of the game's finest endgame theoreticians and commentators. He has contributed regular columns to national broadsheets, including *The Observer* and *The Independent*, as well as *CHESS Magazine*.

His notable books include:
* *Analysing the Endgame* (1981)
* *Endgame Preparation* (1981)
* *Batsford Chess Endings* (1993, co-author)
* *Jon Speelman's Best Games* (1997)
