# Jonathan Mestel

**Professor Andrew Jonathan Mestel** (born 13 March 1957 in Cambridge, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, applied mathematician, and World Chess Solving Champion.

## Career Overview
The son of renowned astrophysicist Leon Mestel, Jonathan Mestel emerged as a generational junior talent in the early 1970s. He earned the International Master (IM) title in 1977 and achieved the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1982. A ferocious and inventive tactician, Mestel won the British Chess Championship three times (1976, 1983, and 1988); his 1976 victory at Portsmouth was notable for a historic run of nine consecutive wins from the start, a championship record. Throughout his chess career, he balanced high-level play with academia, later becoming Professor of Applied Mathematics at Imperial College London.

## International Competition
* **Chess Olympiads:** Represented England at six Olympiads between 1976 and 1988, contributing to England's team **silver medals** in 1984 and 1988 and a team **bronze medal** in 1976. At the 1984 Thessaloniki Olympiad, he won an individual **gold medal** for an outstanding score of 7/9 (78%).
* **World Titles & Problem Solving:** Mestel was the first person in chess history to be awarded FIDE Grandmaster titles in both over-the-board play (1982) and chess problem solving (1997). In 1974, he won the inaugural World Cadet Championship (Under-16) at Pont-Sainte-Maxence, and in 1997 he became the individual **World Chess Solving Champion**.
* **Team Honours:** Member of the English team that took gold at the 1978 World Student Team Championship in Mexico City, and helped Great Britain secure team titles at the World Chess Solving Championships in 1986 and 2007.

## Mathematics and Other Pursuits
Alongside his chess achievements, Mestel pursued a distinguished career in applied mathematics. He earned his doctorate at the University of Cambridge with research on the magnetic levitation of liquid metals and currently serves as Professor of Applied Mathematics at Imperial College London, specialising in magnetohydrodynamics and biological fluid mechanics.

An adept mind-sports player, he has also represented England internationally in contract bridge.
