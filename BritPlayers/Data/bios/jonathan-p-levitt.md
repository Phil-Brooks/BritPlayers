# Jonathan Levitt

**Jonathan Paul Levitt** (born 3 June 1963 in Southwark, London, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, author, coach, problemist, and mathematician.

## Career Overview
Levitt emerged as a prominent talent during the British chess boom of the 1970s and 1980s. A mathematics graduate of Magdalen College, Oxford (1982–1985), he was awarded the International Master (IM) title in 1984 and the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1994. He reached a peak FIDE rating of 2495 in January 1989. Known for his deep analytical ability and intellectual curiosity, Levitt established a multifaceted career that went beyond standard tournament play to embrace endgame composition, chess psychology, and aesthetics. After stepping back from full-time competitive over-the-board play due to health reasons (an arthritic condition), he successfully applied his mathematical faculties to professional cricket betting, while continuing his creative pursuits in writing and music.

## International and Tournament Successes
* **International Team Representation:** Represented England in international competition, notably playing for the England squad that achieved a historic victory over the Soviet Union at the 1990 Visa Chess Summit in Reykjavik, as well as representing the national team at the 2003 European Team Chess Championship.
* **Tournament Victories:** Won the 2005 Howard Staunton Memorial Tournament at Simpson's-in-the-Strand in London, finishing equal first with Grandmaster Jon Speelman and prevailing on tiebreak. He also took first place at the Vera Menchik Memorial in Maidstone (1994).
* **Notable Performances:** Earned strong finishes in numerous international open and invitational events, including joint second at the Oxford International (1984), contention at the sharp end of the 1995 British Chess Championship (leading late into the tournament), and sustained play in the British 4NCL.

## Composition, Writing, and Intellectual Contributions
Levitt is celebrated for bridging the gap between practical tournament play, the art of composition, and the psychological theories underlying chess strength:
* **Chess Composition & Aesthetics:** A recognized composer and judge of endgame studies and problems, winning multiple prizes for his compositions. In 1996 and 1997, he founded and organized the Batsford Schools Problem Solving Championship to foster artistic and combinatorial problem-solving among young players.
* **Teaching & Coaching:** Taught chess at the City of London School from 1988 to 2005 and worked as an analyst and mentor to several of Great Britain's leading junior talents.
* **Journalism & Digital Media:** Authored regular chess columns for *Oxford Today*, wrote analytical and anecdotal features for Garry Kasparov's *KasparovChess* website, and contributed thought-provoking articles to journals like *Kingpin*.
* **Authorship & Theoretical Concepts:** Levitt has authored seminal books on chess artistry, cognitive limits, and humor:
  * *Bobby Fischer's $5,000,000 Comeback: Fischer–Spassky 1992* (1992, co-authored with Malcolm Pein)
  * *Secrets of Spectacular Chess* (1995; 2nd ed. 2008, co-authored with David Friedgood) – widely considered an aesthetic classic exploring the elements of beauty and artistic themes in both over-the-board play and endgame studies
  * *Genius in Chess* (1997) – an examination of human intelligence, innate talent, and calculation, which introduced the widely referenced "Levitt Equation" comparing IQ potential with Elo rating
  * *Contemplating Comedy* (2020) – a foray outside of chess exploring the philosophy and psychology of humor
