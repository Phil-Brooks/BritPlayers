# Julian Hodgson

**Julian Michael Hodgson** (born 25 July 1963 in London, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, four-time British Champion, and author, best known for his swashbuckling, aggressive tactical style and for revitalising the Trompowsky Attack into a mainstream international opening weapon.

## Career Overview
Hodgson first emerged in the 1970s as a junior prodigy at Hammersmith Chess Club in West London, capturing the London Under-18 Championship at age 12 and the British Boys Under-21 title at just 14. A prominent figure of the "English Chess Explosion," he attained the International Master (IM) title in 1983 and earned his Grandmaster (GM) title in 1988. Known for his rapid, uncompromising attacks—which earned him the affectionate moniker "The Grandmaster of Disaster"—Hodgson established a dominant domestic record, winning the British Chess Championship four times (1991, 1992, 1999, and 2000). His 1992 title was achieved with a dominant 10/11 score. Known equally for his relaxed, eccentric persona, at the 2000 championship he famously brought his own swivel executive armchair into the playing hall to wheel between boards. He reached a peak FIDE rating of 2640 in July 2000 and peaked as world No. 27 in 1993, before largely retiring from competitive play in the early 2000s to focus on teaching.

## International Competition
* **Chess Olympiads and Team Events:** Represented England in multiple major international team competitions. At the Chess Olympiads, he won a team **bronze medal** at Novi Sad 1990 and an **individual silver medal** on board six at Manila 1992. He also represented England at the European Team Chess Championship, securing an individual board **gold medal** at Haifa 1989 and helping England capture a historic team **gold medal** at Pula 1997.
* **Tournament Successes:** Won or shared first place at numerous strong international events across Europe and North America, including Benidorm (1986), the Seville Open (1986, 1988), the Geneva Open (1988), Kecskemét (1988), Dos Hermanas (1989), San Bernardino (1989), Cappelle-la-Grande (1992), the Canadian Open Championship (1997), and the National Open in Las Vegas (1997). He was also runner-up at the Lloyds Bank Masters (1986) and the Philadelphia World Open (1990).

## Playing Style and Opening Legacy
Hodgson is renowned for an uncompromisingly aggressive, imaginative style that aimed to avoid sterile theoretical memorisation—a choice he humorously attributed to laziness—in favour of early initiative and sharp complications. His most lasting contribution to chess theory was resurrecting the previously fringe **Trompowsky Attack** (1.d4 Nf6 2.Bg5), developing it into a respected, aggressive system adopted at the highest levels; Grandmaster Joe Gallagher famously remarked that it should be called the "Hodgson–Trompowsky Attack". The sister system 1.d4 d5 2.Bg5 is similarly dubbed the **Hodgson Attack** (or Pseudo-Trompowsky). Outside tournament play, Hodgson was a popular commentator, lecturer, and prolific writer who authored several popular instructional books focusing on attacking technique and his pet opening lines.

His notable published works include:
* *Grand Prix Attack: f4 Against the Sicilian* (1985)
* *Chess Traveller's Quiz Book* (1993)
* *Quick Chess Knockouts* (1996)
* *Attack with GM Julian Hodgson, Vol. 1* (1996)
* *Attack with GM Julian Hodgson, Vol. 2* (1997)
* *Secrets of the Trompovsky* (1997)
