# Michael Adams

**Michael Adams** (born 17 November 1971 in Truro, Cornwall, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, nine-time British Champion, and former World Championship finalist, widely regarded as one of the strongest and most accomplished players in British chess history.

## Career Overview
Adams was a child prodigy who quickly rose through junior ranks after learning the game at age six. Coached early on by former European Junior Champion Shaun Taulbut, he won the Cornwall County Under-9, Under-13, Under-15, and Under-18 championships by the time he was nine years old. In 1987, he earned a silver medal at the World Under-16 Championship and became the world's youngest International Master at the time. In 1989, at age 17, he earned his Grandmaster (GM) title and became the youngest player ever to win the British Chess Championship. Adams went on to dominate the national stage across four decades, winning nine British titles (1989, 1997, 2010, 2011, 2016, 2018, 2019, 2023, and 2025) and constructing an astonishing 118-game unbeaten streak in classical British Championship play. He reached an elite peak classical rating of 2761—the highest in English chess history—and ranked as high as world No. 4 repeatedly between 2000 and 2002. In 2023, he captured the World Senior Chess Championship (50+ category).

## International Competition
* **World Championship Cycle:** Established himself as one of the fiercest knockout tournament competitors in modern history. He reached the semifinals of the FIDE World Chess Championship in 1997, 1999, and 2000. His career peak in the cycle came at the 2004 FIDE World Championship in Tripoli, where he advanced to the final, narrowly missing the world title in rapid tiebreaks against Rustam Kasimdzhanov. He subsequently competed in the eight-player 2005 FIDE World Championship tournament in San Luis.
* **Chess Olympiads and Team Events:** Served as the long-time bedrock of the English national team, featuring in every Chess Olympiad between 1990 and 2024, predominantly on top boards. He won team **bronze** at the 1990 Novi Sad Olympiad and an individual board three **bronze** at Istanbul 2000. At the European Team Chess Championship, he propelled England to a historic team **gold medal** at Pula 1997 and captured three individual board **gold medals** across his international appearances.
* **Tournament Successes:** Triumphed at many of the world's premier elite super-tournaments, including Tilburg (1992), Dos Hermanas (1995, 1999), the PCA Candidates Qualifier at Groningen (1993), the Howard Staunton Memorial (2007, 2008), the Gibraltar Chess Festival (2010), the World Open (2011), the Dortmund Sparkassen Chess Meeting (2013), and the London Chess Classic (2023).

## Playing Style and Publications
Nicknamed "The Spider", Adams is celebrated for his smooth, harmonious positional style, clean technique, and uncanny intuition. Opponents frequently commented that his plans appeared so natural and subtle that they only realized they were trapped in a web when it was too late to escape. Blessed with exceptional economy of movement, profound prophylaxis, and virtuoso endgame technique, he has consistently scored against generations of top-ten players. Adams has shared his games, career reflections, and decision-making methodology in several acclaimed chess books.

His notable published works include:
* *Development of a Grandmaster* (1991, with Bill Adams)
* *Chess in the Fast Lane* (1996, with Bill Adams)
* *Think Like a Super-GM* (2022, with Philip Hurtado)
