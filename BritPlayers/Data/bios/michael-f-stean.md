# Michael Stean

**Michael Francis Stean** (born 4 September 1953 in London, England) is an English chess Grandmaster, author, and tax accountant.

## Career Overview
Stean was educated at Latymer Upper School and read mathematics at Trinity College, Cambridge. A standout junior talent, he tied for first place in the 1974 British Chess Championship, narrowly losing the play-off match to George Botterill. He earned the International Master (IM) title in 1975 and in 1977 became England's third Grandmaster (GM), following Tony Miles and Raymond Keene. In 1982, at age 29 and at the height of his playing career, Stean retired from competitive chess to work as a tax accountant.

## International Competition
* **Chess Olympiads:** Represented England at five consecutive Olympiads (1974, 1976, 1978, 1980, and 1982). On his debut at Nice in 1974, he won the prize for best game of the Olympiad for his victory over Walter Browne. At Haifa in 1976, he won the individual **gold medal** on board four while helping England secure a historic team **bronze medal**.
* **World Championship Second:** Served as an indispensable second and opening theoretician for Viktor Korchnoi during his 1977–78 and 1980–81 Candidates victories and World Championship matches against Anatoly Karpov.
* **Tournament Successes:** Took first place at international tournaments including Vršac (1979), Smederevska Palanka (1980), and Beersheba (1982), alongside strong podium finishes at Montilla (1976, 1977) and London (1977).

## Writing and Later Career
Between 1979 and 1993, Stean served as the regular chess columnist for *The Observer*. He wrote two widely praised books:
* *Sicilian: Najdorf* (1976)
* *Simple Chess* (1978) – an enduring instructional classic celebrated for demystifying core positional strategies and pawn structures

Following his retirement from competitive play in 1982, he established a long-running professional career as an international tax specialist.
