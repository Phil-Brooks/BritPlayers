# Paul Motwani

**Paul Anthony Motwani** (born 13 June 1962 in Glasgow, Scotland) is a Scottish chess Grandmaster, author, journalist, and mathematics educator. He holds a historic place in Scottish chess as the country's first-ever Grandmaster.

## Career Overview
Motwani rose to international prominence in 1978 when he won the World Cadet (Under-17) Championship. That same year, at the age of 16, he became the youngest player to that point to win the Scottish Chess Championship. After earning a degree in mathematics and physics at the University of Dundee, he worked for several years as a secondary school mathematics teacher before taking time out to focus on chess at the highest level. Motwani earned the International Master (IM) title in 1987 and made history in 1992 by becoming Scotland's first Grandmaster (GM). Over a competitive career spanning several decades, he reached a peak FIDE rating of 2552 in July 2004. Alongside competitive play, he built an enduring reputation as an educator, columnist, and author of innovative chess instruction books.

## International and Tournament Successes
* **Historic Milestone & World Title:** Crowned World Cadet (Under-17) Champion in 1978 in Sas van Gent, Netherlands, and officially became Scotland's inaugural Grandmaster in 1992.
* **Scottish National Championships:** Won the Scottish Chess Championship title seven times (1978, 1986, 1987, 1992, shared 1993, 2002, and shared 2003).
* **Olympiad & Team Representation:** Represented Scotland at nine Chess Olympiads (1986, 1988, 1990, 1992, 1994, 1996, 2000, 2002, and 2004). He achieved his first two Grandmaster norms at the Dubai (1986) and Thessaloniki (1988) Olympiads, securing his third and final norm at the Manila Olympiad in 1992.
* **Notable Scalps:** Over his career, secured victories over numerous top-tier Grandmasters, including Michael Adams, Nigel Short, Joël Lautier, Vladimir Akopian, and Miguel Illescas.

## Writing, Journalism, and Education
Motwani is noted for his lively, mnemonic-driven teaching approach and his dedication to popularizing the game:
* **Journalism & Media:** Penned a long-running weekly chess column for *The Scotsman* and contributed extensively to *Scottish Chess* (the official magazine of Chess Scotland). In 1992, he and fellow Dundee Grandmaster Colin McNab were profiled in the Grampian Television documentary *The Grandmasters of Dundee*.
* **Mathematics & Chess Teaching:** Known affectionately as "Mr. Mo," Motwani has maintained a dual vocation in teaching, conducting educational sessions combining mathematics, chess, and puzzles in schools and clubs.
* **Authorship:** Authored several popular, highly creative instructional books published by Batsford, Gambit, and Everyman, including:
  * *H.O.T. Chess* (1996)
  * *C.O.O.L. Chess* (1997)
  * *S.T.A.R. Chess* (1998)
  * *Chess Under the Microscope* (1998)
  * *The Most Instructive Games of the Young Grandmasters* (1999)
