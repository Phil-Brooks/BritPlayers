# Raymond Keene

**Raymond Dennis Keene OBE** (born 29 January 1948 in London, England) is a chess Grandmaster, author, columnist, and organiser.

## Career Overview
Keene was educated at Dulwich College and Trinity College, Cambridge. He won the British Under-18 Championship in 1964 and went on to win the British Chess Championship in 1971. He was awarded the International Master (IM) title in 1972 and in 1974 became the first English player to achieve a Grandmaster norm. In 1976, he became England's second Grandmaster (GM), shortly following Tony Miles. Keene stepped back from competitive play in 1986 to focus on organizing, journalism, and broadcasting.

## International Competition
* **Chess Olympiads:** Represented England in eight consecutive Chess Olympiads between 1966 and 1980. He earned an individual bronze medal on board four at Lugano (1968) and helped England achieve a historic team **bronze medal** at Haifa (1976), where he also took an individual bronze medal on board two.
* **World Championship Second:** Served as an instrumental second to Viktor Korchnoi during the controversial 1978 World Chess Championship match against Anatoly Karpov in Baguio, and later assisted Garry Kasparov during the 1986 World Championship match.
* **Tournament Successes:** Won or tied for first at notable international events including Alicante (1977), Sydney (1979), Dortmund (1980), and Adelaide (1980).

## Writing and Event Organisation
Keene was one of chess's most prominent media figures, serving as the long-running chess columnist for *The Spectator* (1977–2019) and *The Times* (1985–2019). As an organiser, he played a central role in staging high-profile events in London, including halves or full matches of the World Chess Championships in 1986, 1993, and 2000, as well as co-founding the **Mind Sports Olympiad** in 1997.

He is one of the game's most prolific writers, having authored or co-authored well over 100 books on chess and thinking, including:
* *Aron Nimzowitsch: A Reappraisal* (1974)
* *Howard Staunton: The English World Chess Champion* (1975)
* *Batsford Chess Openings* (1982, with Garry Kasparov)

In the 1985 Birthday Honours, Keene was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) for services to chess.
