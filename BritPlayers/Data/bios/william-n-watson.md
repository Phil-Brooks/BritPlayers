# William Watson

**William Nicholas Watson** (born 18 April 1962 in Baghdad, Iraq) is an English chess Grandmaster, former British Champion, and corporate lawyer.

## Career Overview
Watson rose to prominence as part of the golden generation of English chess in the late 1970s and 1980s, gaining the International Master (IM) title in 1982 and the Grandmaster (GM) title in 1990. Renowned for his fierce, highly tactical, and uncompromising style, he reached the pinnacle of domestic chess by winning the British Rapidplay Championship in 1992 and the British Chess Championship in 1994. He attained a peak FIDE rating of 2560 in July 1992, placing him comfortably among the world's top 100 players. Watson subsequently transitioned from full-time chess into commercial law, becoming a leading tax partner at the elite "Magic Circle" firm Slaughter and May while remaining an occasional competitor and captain in team events.

## International and Tournament Successes
* **International Team Representation:** Represented England at the 1988 Chess Olympiad in Thessaloniki, securing the team **silver medal** alongside Nigel Short, Jon Speelman, John Nunn, Murray Chandler, and Jonathan Mestel. He also represented England at the 1989 European Team Chess Championship in Haifa.
* **National Championship Titles:** Won the British Rapidplay Championship in 1992 and captured the British Chess Championship in Norwich in 1994.
* **Tournament Victories:** Won the Philips & Drew Knights tournament in London (1982), shared first place at Bor (1986), tied for first at Kecskemét Dutep (1988), shared victory at the London WFW tournament (1991), and won the Prague Chip tournament outright (1992).
* **Notable Podiums:** Finished joint second at Brighton (1983), second at the Oslo Cup (1991), second at Herning (1991), and third at the Gausdal International (1991).
* **Club and League Competitions:** Competed extensively in the German Bundesliga—primarily representing SV Castrop-Rauxel (1993–2002) and later Munich MSC 1836—and participated in the European Club Cup representing Barbican Chess Club.

## Playing Style, Mentorship, and Legal Career
Watson is notable both for his distinctive over-the-board reputation and for executing a successful transition into top-tier corporate law:
* **"Drunk with a Machine Gun":** Former World Champion Boris Spassky famously characterized Watson's razor-sharp, chaotic, and relentlessly attacking approach as that of "a drunk with a machine gun"—an homage to his eagerness to steer games into rich, tactical melee regardless of the opponent.
* **Seconding and Captaincy:** Served as second to GM Jon Speelman during the 1988 Candidates cycle and Interzonal matches in Saint John, New Brunswick. Decades later, he returned to the national squad in an administrative leadership capacity, serving as England's team captain at the 45th Chess Olympiad.
* **Education and Legal Practice:** Educated at Merton College, Oxford—where he played board one during Oxford's dominant run in the annual Varsity matches against Cambridge—Watson pursued a career in law after stepping back from professional chess. He became a specialist in corporate tax and transactions, joining Slaughter and May where he was made a partner in 2004.
